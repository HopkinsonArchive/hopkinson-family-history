> [!summary] Profile
> **Born:** February 19, 1648/49, Rowley, Massachusetts Bay Colony  
> **Died:** April 17, 1721, Bradford, Massachusetts Bay Province  
> **Parents:** [[Michael Hopkinson]] and [[Ann — wife of Michael Hopkinson|Ann]]  
> **Spouses:** [[Sarah Wallingford]]; [[Sarah Wheeler Spofford]]  
> **Direct-line child:** [[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]]  
> **Evidence status:** Confirmed

Caleb Hopkinson Sr. was the youngest documented son of [[Michael Hopkinson]], an early settler of Rowley, and Michael's wife Ann. Born only days before his father's death, Caleb grew up in a blended household headed successively by his mother’s later husbands. He eventually settled in Bradford, became a property-owning freeholder, administered his first father-in-law’s estate, and was trusted by the Essex County court to help divide another Bradford family’s houses and lands.

## Birth and parentage

The Rowley vital record identifies Caleb as the son of “Michaell and Ann” and records his birth as:

> “19 : 12m : 1648”

Under the calendar then used in Massachusetts, March was the first month and February was the twelfth. The date therefore converts to **February 19, 1648/49**, not December 19, 1648 as some online trees report.

Caleb’s father died in late February 1648/49, only days after Caleb’s birth. Caleb therefore could not have personally remembered Michael.

**Evidence status: Confirmed.**

## Childhood and blended family

After Michael’s death, Ann married [[John Trumble]] in August 1650 and later [[Richard Swan]] on March 1, 1658/59. Caleb consequently grew up with his surviving Hopkinson brothers and his younger Trumble half-sisters in the Trumble and Swan households.

Ann’s 1678 will left Caleb household furnishings and a chest connected to his father. A surviving transcription reads:

> “I Give My Son Caleb Hopkinson the Old Bed in the Chamber & Bolster & Cotton Rugg & Blankett & a chest that his father Gott made.”

The most natural reading is that Michael had obtained or commissioned the chest. The wording does not establish that Michael personally constructed it. The original manuscript should be checked to confirm the exact reading of “Gott made.”

The chest is one of the few tangible objects presently associated with the immigrant generation of the family.

**Evidence status: Confirmed bequest; exact wording and meaning require manuscript verification.**

## Connection to Richard Swan’s estate

The probate accounting for Caleb’s stepfather, Richard Swan, includes a five-shilling entry involving Caleb and [[Joseph Kilburne]]. Joseph was the husband of Caleb’s Trumble half-sister Mary. This places Caleb and his brother-in-law within the financial settlement of the Swan household, although the surviving abstract does not establish the reason for the payment.

**Evidence status: Confirmed association; purpose of the payment unresolved.**

## First marriage: Sarah Wallingford

Caleb married [[Sarah Wallingford]] in Bradford on November 25, 1679. Sarah, born May 20, 1661, was a daughter of [[Nicholas Wallingford]] and [[Sarah Travers Wallingford]].

Their only presently documented child was:

- [[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]], born January 7, 1681, Bradford

Sarah died on February 9, 1682. Their son was approximately thirteen months old, leaving Caleb a widower with an infant child.

**Evidence status: Confirmed.**

## Administrator of the Wallingford estate

Following Nicholas Wallingford’s death around 1680–81, the Essex County court appointed Nicholas’s widow, Sarah Travers Wallingford, and his son-in-law Caleb Hopkinson to administer the estate. They were ordered to return an inventory. The court later permitted them to sell enough estate land to raise approximately £40, apparently to meet the estate’s obligations.

This proceeding shows Caleb handling land, debts, and inheritance by his early thirties. It also indicates that the Wallingford family and the court considered him capable of significant legal and financial responsibility.

The settlement coincided with an exceptionally difficult period: Caleb’s first wife died in 1682 while their son was still an infant and her father’s estate was being resolved.

**Evidence status: Confirmed through Essex County court and probate abstracts.**

## Bradford freeholder and court appointee

By 1697 Caleb was an established Bradford landowner. On November 23 of that year, the Salem court appointed five “freeholders in Bradford” to divide the houses and lands of the late [[Samuel Worcester]] among his surviving children:

- Captain [[David Haseltine]]
- Ensign [[Joseph Bailey]]
- Corporal [[John Boynton]]
- Mr. [[Richard Kimball]]
- Mr. **Caleb Hopkinson**

The panel was instructed first to set out £100 for the estate’s administrators, William and Francis Worcester, and then divide the remaining property among the heirs.

The appointment establishes more than simple residence. Caleb owned real property and possessed enough local standing to be entrusted by the county court with evaluating and dividing another family’s real estate. The record styles him “Mr. Caleb Hopkinson,” a respectful designation in that period.

**Evidence status: Confirmed.**

## Second marriage: Sarah Wheeler Spofford

After approximately nineteen years as a widower, Caleb married [[Sarah Wheeler Spofford]] in Bradford on June 12, 1701. Sarah was the widow of [[John Spofford]], not his daughter. She brought several children from her first marriage into the household.

In 1705 Caleb’s son, [[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]], married Sarah’s daughter [[Martha Spofford]]. Caleb Jr. and Martha had become stepsiblings through their parents’ 1701 marriage, but they were not biologically related.

No dependable evidence has yet been found that Caleb Sr. and Sarah Wheeler had a child together. Online claims assigning them a son named Ebenezer in 1717 are presently **unverified and probably erroneous**.

**Evidence status: Second marriage confirmed; any children of the marriage unresolved.**

## Death and burial

Caleb died in Bradford on April 17, 1721, aged seventy-two. His gravestone was transcribed as:

> Here Lyes Buried  
> The Body of Clark  
> Caleb Hopkinson  
> who Died April  
> 17 1721 in  
> The 73 year of  
> His Age

“In the 73 year of his age” agrees with his documented birth: Caleb was seventy-two and had entered his seventy-third year.

The word transcribed as **“Clark”** has been interpreted as **“Clerk.”** It may identify a civic, parish, or recordkeeping role, but no record has yet been located proving that Caleb served as Bradford town clerk. The inscription and its meaning should therefore be preserved without assigning him a specific office.

Caleb was buried in Bradford’s Ancient Burying Ground. His widow Sarah survived him and died on October 24, 1732.

**Evidence status: Death and inscription confirmed; meaning of “Clark” unresolved.**

## Historical assessment

The surviving records portray Caleb as an established member of early Bradford rather than merely a name in a lineage. He was a freeholder, participated in probate administration, and was selected by the county court to divide real property. His family relationships connected the Hopkinsons with the Trumble, Swan, Kilburne, Wallingford, Wheeler, and Spofford families of the Rowley–Bradford area.

The direct Hopkinson line continues through his only documented son:

**[[Michael Hopkinson]] → Caleb Hopkinson Sr. → [[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]]**

### Confirmed

- Son of Michael and Ann Hopkinson
- Born February 19, 1648/49 in Rowley
- Father died during Caleb’s infancy
- Received household goods and a chest associated with Michael under Ann’s will
- Settled in Bradford
- Married Sarah Wallingford in 1679
- Father of Caleb Hopkinson Jr.
- Helped administer Nicholas Wallingford’s estate
- Identified as a Bradford freeholder in 1697
- Appointed by the Salem court to help divide Samuel Worcester’s real estate
- Married widow Sarah Wheeler Spofford in 1701
- Died April 17, 1721 in Bradford
- Gravestone described him as “Clark” Caleb Hopkinson

### Unresolved

- Caleb’s occupation
- The precise location and extent of his Bradford property
- Whether “Clark” meant town clerk, parish clerk, or another role
- Caleb’s own will, inventory, or complete probate file
- The original manuscript reading of the chest bequest
- Whether any children resulted from his second marriage
