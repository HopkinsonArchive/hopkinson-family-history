# The Hopkinson Family Archive

This site documents more than four centuries of Hopkinson family history through genealogical research, biographies, military records, censuses, vital records, town histories, newspapers, and surviving family evidence.

The presently reconstructed family line extends from **Michael Hopkinson in seventeenth-century Massachusetts** to his descendants in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

> [!important] Research in progress
> This is a documentary research archive, not a claim that every published relationship or biographical detail has been proven conclusively. Evidence is classified as **confirmed**, **strongly inferred**, **unverified**, or **unresolved**. Conclusions will be corrected when stronger records are found.

## An Important Lineage Distinction

Lowell Herbert Hopkinson’s surviving Bradford birth record identifies him as the son of the unmarried **Ann Matilda Hopkinson**. It does not name his biological father.

Lowell inherited and retained his mother’s surname. The family’s genealogical descent from the colonial Hopkinsons therefore continues through Ann Matilda, with the **Hopkinson surname transmitted maternally at this generation**.

Evidence concerning Lowell’s possible biological father is examined separately and is identified according to its evidentiary strength. The complete lineage should not be described as an uninterrupted male-line or Y-chromosome Hopkinson descent.

## Featured Family History

### [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]]

Civil War soldier, shoemaker, former prisoner of war, and later a severely disabled veteran whose life is documented through military, census, pension, and newspaper evidence.

### [[Ann Matilda Hopkinson]]

Lowell’s mother and the crucial link through whom the colonial Hopkinson ancestry and surname passed to subsequent generations.

### [[Uriah Hopkinson]]

Ann Matilda’s father and a central figure in Lowell’s childhood household. His occupation, household history, and relationship with Lowell remain active areas of research.

## How Evidence Is Classified

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Supported by primary or highly reliable contemporary records |
| **Strong inference** | The available evidence strongly supports the conclusion, but direct proof has not been found |
| **Unverified** | Reported in family history or secondary sources but not independently confirmed |
| **Unresolved** | Evidence is missing, incomplete, or conflicting |

Original vital records, censuses, probate files, military records, church records, town records, cemetery records, and contemporary newspapers receive priority. Published genealogies and online family trees are treated as research leads unless independently supported.
