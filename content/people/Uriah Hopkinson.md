> [!warning] A documented life with an unresolved ending
> Uriah is well documented in Bradford and East Bradford records from his birth through the middle of the nineteenth century. His late-life record is substantially weaker. A reported death on **February 20, 1876, in Groveland** has not been confirmed in the surviving local death compilation, no gravestone for him appears in the published transcription of the old Groveland cemetery, and no probate file has yet been located. A page-by-page examination of the original **1870 Groveland federal census** also found no household headed by, or clearly containing, Uriah Hopkinson. These absences do not prove when or where he died, but they are too significant to conceal behind an apparently certain 1876 date.

**Uriah Hopkinson** was born in Bradford, Massachusetts, on **September 28, 1791**, the son of [[Silas Hopkinson]] and [[Hannah Balch]]. He followed a skilled woodworking trade and was identified as a **joiner**. His house stood beside the Merrimack River at a location later remembered as one of Groveland's vessel-building sites. Uriah married first [[Judith Latham]], who died on the day their daughter Judith was born, and then [[Lucy Chase]], with whom he had the larger recorded family that included direct-line daughter [[Ann Matilda Hopkinson]].

Uriah was Ann Matilda's father and the maternal grandfather of [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]]. His household belongs to the family setting in which Lowell's early life is reconstructed, but the precise dates, legal arrangement, and daily circumstances of Uriah's role in raising Lowell have not yet been established from a guardianship record or a complete sequence of household censuses.

## Birth and family background

The published Bradford vital records identify **Uriah, son of Silas and Hannah Hopkinson**, as born on **September 28, 1791**.

His father, [[Silas Hopkinson]], was a house carpenter, building contractor, and tobacco manufacturer. Silas helped construct the East Parish meetinghouse in 1790 and was sufficiently established in his trade to train an apprentice. Uriah's later identification as a joiner therefore places him in a closely related skilled woodworking tradition, although no surviving apprenticeship record proves that Silas personally trained him.

The direct line is:

**[[Samuel Hopkinson]] → [[Silas Hopkinson]] → Uriah Hopkinson → [[Ann Matilda Hopkinson]] → [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]]**

## First marriage: Judith Latham

Uriah married **Judith Latham** in Bradford on **November 6, 1812**.

Their daughter **Judith Latham Hopkinson** was born on **September 26, 1813**. A cemetery inscription records that Judith, the consort of Uriah Hopkinson, died on that same date at the age of twenty-three.

The exact agreement between the child's birth date and the mother's death date strongly suggests that Judith died in childbirth or from an immediate complication of delivery. No surviving record found so far states a medical cause, so that explanation remains a **strong inference**, not a confirmed diagnosis.

Uriah was left a widower at approximately twenty-one years of age with an infant daughter.

## Second marriage: Lucy Chase

Uriah married **Lucy Chase** in Bradford on **June 7, 1814**. The marriage record explicitly describes him as a widower, connecting this marriage to the death of Judith Latham.

Lucy was born on **January 10, 1792**, a daughter of **Nathaniel Lowe Chase** and **Lydia Dustin**. Through Lydia, Lucy descended from the Dustin family remembered in northern Massachusetts history.

The later life and death of Lucy remain unresolved. No matching death entry or gravestone has yet been securely identified, and she has not been placed with confidence in a late-life census household.

## Children

Bradford records and later compilations associate the following children with Uriah:

1. **Judith Latham Hopkinson**, born September 26, 1813, daughter of Uriah and Judith Latham.
2. **[[Ann Matilda Hopkinson]]**, born November 15, 1817, daughter of Uriah and Lucy; next direct-line ancestor.
3. **George W. Hopkinson**, born about 1820; identified at his 1846 marriage as a twenty-six-year-old shoemaker and son of Uriah and Lucy.
4. **Adaline Chase Hopkinson**, born October 20, 1826, daughter of Uriah and Lucy; married Paul Hopkinson Jr. on May 14, 1846.
5. **Joseph Hay Hopkinson**, born August 17, 1831; the Bradford birth entry identifies Uriah as his father but does not name the mother in the compiled record.

A later Balch genealogy lists a daughter **Hannah** but omits the documented daughter Judith. That list should not be accepted without qualification. Hannah may represent an additional child, a mistaken substitution for Judith, or confusion with Uriah's sister Hannah Hopkinson. Her identity is **unresolved**.

## A multigenerational household

The 1840 federal pension census places **Lydia Chase**, aged eighty-five, in Uriah Hopkinson's household. Lydia was Lucy's mother.

This is direct evidence that Uriah and Lucy maintained a multigenerational home and cared for an elderly parent. It also provides important context for the later family understanding that their grandson Lowell belonged within the Hopkinson household network.

However, the evidence must be kept within its limits. Lydia's presence in 1840 does not by itself prove the dates or terms under which Uriah and Lucy raised Lowell. No guardianship appointment, custody agreement, or uninterrupted set of household schedules has yet been found. Uriah's importance in Lowell's upbringing is strongly supported by the reconstructed family setting, but the specific arrangement remains undocumented.

## Joiner and woodworker

A federal document identifies **“Uriah Hopkinson, joiner.”** A joiner was a skilled woodworker who made fitted wooden components and finished work, a trade closely related to carpentry.

This occupational evidence is important because Uriah has sometimes been considered a possible source of Lowell's later shoemaking trade. The surviving record does not support that claim. Uriah was a joiner, while other members of the family were explicitly identified as shoemakers:

- Uriah's son **George W. Hopkinson** was a shoemaker in 1846.
- Uriah's son-in-law **Paul Hopkinson Jr.** was also a shoemaker.

Either man could have belonged to the trade network through which Lowell learned shoemaking, but no apprenticeship or employment record connects Lowell to either one. The question remains unresolved.

## House beside the Merrimack shipyards

Later Groveland history places **Scanty Point nearly opposite Uriah Hopkinson's house** and states that vessels were built **in front of Uriah Hopkinson's house**.

This locates Uriah's home along the Merrimack River beside a documented vessel-building area. The location fits his woodworking occupation and makes some participation in local ship or boat construction plausible. The wording of the local history identifies the site by his house; it does not state that Uriah owned the yard, built vessels, or worked there.

Accordingly:

- **Confirmed:** Uriah's house was used as a landmark for a vessel-building location.
- **Strong inference:** His riverside surroundings brought him into regular contact with the local building economy.
- **Unresolved:** Whether he performed joinery on vessels, owned waterfront land, or participated financially in shipbuilding.

## The flood of 1846

A contemporary newspaper report of the severe Merrimack River flood of **March 1846** named Uriah's dwelling among the affected Groveland-area houses. Water reached the principal floor or flooded the lower part of the dwelling.

The report confirms that the riverside location was not merely descriptive. Uriah and his household directly experienced one of the major environmental hazards of nineteenth-century life along the Merrimack.

The surviving report does not give the cost of the damage, identify lost possessions, or state how long the family was displaced.

## The break in Uriah's late-life record

Uriah's biography presently has a substantial documentary break after the middle decades of the nineteenth century.

### 1870 census search

The original 1870 federal population schedule for Groveland was examined page by page. The town occupies forty-five written census pages on Massachusetts roll M593-608. Numerous Hopkinson households appear, but **Uriah was not found by name**, and no household could be identified confidently as Uriah and Lucy under variant given names.

One household contains an eighty-year-old **William Hopkinson** and a sixty-eight-year-old **Adeline Hopkinson**. The written name appears to be William, and the woman's name and age do not match Lucy. That household should not be reassigned to Uriah merely to close the gap.

The negative result leaves several possibilities:

1. Uriah lived outside Groveland in 1870.
2. He was omitted from the enumeration.
3. He was recorded under an unexpected or badly written name.
4. The reported 1876 death date or place has been attached to the wrong identity.

None of these explanations has yet been proven.

### Reported death in 1876

Commercially indexed material reports that Uriah died in Groveland on **February 20, 1876**. The original state death return has not been located and inspected.

The surviving Groveland vital-record compilation, which includes other members of the Hopkinson family, contains no matching entry for Uriah. This omission does not disprove the commercial index, because local compilations can be incomplete, but it prevents the date from being treated as confirmed.

### No identified burial or probate

The published 1895 transcription of the old Groveland cemetery records the graves of many Hopkinsons, including Uriah's first wife Judith, but does not record a gravestone for Uriah or Lucy.

No probate case has yet been identified for Uriah in the publicly searchable Essex County material. Absence from a simple search is not proof that no estate proceeding occurred; docket indexes and original packets require further examination.

> [!important] Present conclusion
> Uriah was certainly born in 1791 and lived into the nineteenth century, but his presence after the middle of the century and the circumstances of his death are not yet documented by a coherent chain of primary records. **February 20, 1876, Groveland** is retained only as a reported lead.

## Historical assessment

The surviving evidence presents Uriah as a skilled Bradford and East Bradford joiner whose life was centered near the Merrimack River. His first marriage ended with the probable childbirth death of Judith Latham. His second household with Lucy Chase included children, an elderly mother-in-law, and the family network into which his grandson Lowell was born.

Uriah's life also illustrates an important research problem. The early and middle portions are supported by vital records, occupational evidence, local history, a pension census, and a contemporary newspaper. The expected records of his final years do not presently align. A responsible biography must preserve both parts: the substantial life that can be reconstructed and the unresolved disappearance from the late-life record.

## Evidence assessment

### Confirmed

- Birth on September 28, 1791, in Bradford
- Parentage as son of Silas Hopkinson and Hannah Balch
- Marriage to Judith Latham on November 6, 1812
- Birth of daughter Judith on September 26, 1813
- Death of wife Judith on the same date
- Status as a widower when he married Lucy Chase on June 7, 1814
- Births or parentage of Ann Matilda, George W., Adaline Chase, and Joseph Hay Hopkinson
- Occupation as a joiner
- Presence of Lucy's mother Lydia Chase in his household in 1840
- Riverside house used as a landmark for a Groveland vessel-building site
- Direct impact of the March 1846 flood on his dwelling
- Absence of a named Uriah Hopkinson household from the examined 1870 Groveland census pages

### Strong inference

- Judith Latham Hopkinson died from childbirth or an immediate complication
- Uriah learned or developed his woodworking trade within the occupational world of his father Silas
- Uriah's household formed an important part of Lowell Herbert Hopkinson's childhood family network
- Uriah's riverside location connected him with Groveland's boat- and shipbuilding economy

### Unverified

- Death on February 20, 1876, in Groveland
- A daughter Hannah in the household
- Direct participation by Uriah in vessel construction
- A role for Uriah in teaching Lowell the shoemaking trade

### Unresolved

- Uriah and Lucy's locations in later federal censuses
- Exact household arrangement through which Lowell was raised
- Lucy's death date and place
- Uriah's original death return
- Uriah's burial place and possible gravestone
- Probate or estate administration
- Deeds establishing ownership, acreage, and precise location of the riverside house
- Reason Uriah is absent from the 1870 Groveland census under his own name
- Whether the reported 1876 death belongs to this Uriah Hopkinson

## Research priorities

1. Locate and inspect the original Massachusetts death return underlying the reported February 20, 1876 entry.
2. Search the complete 1850, 1860, and 1870 federal indexes beyond Groveland, including variant and mistranscribed surnames.
3. Examine Essex County probate docket indexes and original case files for 1870–1880.
4. Search Essex County grantor and grantee deed indexes for Uriah and Lucy Hopkinson.
5. Trace Lucy separately through death, cemetery, census, church, and estate records.
6. Search for guardianship, school, apprenticeship, poor-relief, or court records concerning Lowell Herbert Hopkinson.
7. Identify the federal document that calls Uriah a joiner within its complete administrative context.
8. Search Groveland and Bradford tax, poll, highway, and town-account records to establish Uriah's last documented residence year.
