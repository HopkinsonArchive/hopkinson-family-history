**Harry Francis Hopkinson** was born at Groveland, Massachusetts, on October 4, 1868, the son of [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]] and [[Margaret (Metcalf) Hopkinson]].[^1] He married [[Flora (Barnhill) Hopkinson]] and became the father of two documented children, Marion Maude and Ralph L. Hopkinson.[^2][^3]

Harry died at Groveland on November 1, 1897, only twenty-nine years old.[^4] His brief adult life is presently known principally through municipal vital records, the town's annual report, and later records concerning his widow and children. No reliable record yet found identifies his occupation or cause of death.

His death was followed ninety-six days later by the death of his five-year-old daughter Marion in Nova Scotia.[^5] By 1901, Flora and the surviving son Ralph were living in Shubenacadie, Nova Scotia, in the household of Flora's parents, Isaac and Hannah Barnhill.[^6]

## Family and relationships

### Parents

Harry's Groveland birth entry identifies him as the son of **Lowell H. Hopkinson and Margaret Metcalf**.[^1] He was born three years after Lowell's final Civil War service and belonged to the postwar household that Lowell and Margaret established in Groveland.

The records examined for Harry do not confuse him with **Herbert L. Hopkinson**, another member of the wider family. Harry's identity is anchored by his exact birth date, his parents, his marriage to Flora, and the births of his children.

### Marriage to Flora Barnhill

Harry married **Flora Barnhill**, a woman associated with Shubenacadie, Nova Scotia. The children's Groveland birth records establish the couple directly as their parents.[^2][^3]

A marginal annotation beside Harry's Groveland birth entry appears to read **“m'd Haverhill 1890.”**[^1] This is strong evidence that a marriage connected with Haverhill occurred around 1890, but the notation is not a substitute for the original marriage return. Until that return is located, the exact date, place, Harry's occupation and residence, and the full information supplied by the bride and groom remain unresolved.

Later records disagree about Flora's birth year. The 1901 Canadian census reports August 14, 1861, while a later secondary index reports August 14, 1864.[^6] The conflict does not affect her identification as Harry's widow and the daughter enumerated in Isaac and Hannah Barnhill's household, but her original Nova Scotia birth or baptismal record is needed to resolve the year.

## Life and historical context

### A Groveland family

Harry's documented life centered on Groveland. He was born there, both children were born there, and he died and was buried there.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^7] The presently available records do not establish a street address or occupation.

Groveland's financial accounting for the period includes a **$2 tax abatement** under Harry's name.[^7] The entry probably represents cancellation of a poll tax following his death and supports his standing as an independently taxable adult resident. Because the account does not state the reason for the abatement, that interpretation remains a **strong inference**. It does not establish an occupation.

### Flora and the children after Harry's death

Marion's cemetery inscription at Preeper Hill in Shubenacadie reads, in transcription, “Marion M., daughter of Harry F. Hopkinson,” and records her death on February 5, 1898, aged five.[^5] Her age and paternal identification agree with the Groveland birth record, confirming that the memorial belongs to the same child.

Province-wide civil death registration in Nova Scotia was not maintained between 1877 and 1908 outside limited local coverage, so a provincial civil certificate for Marion may not exist.[^9] A church register, contemporary newspaper, or original cemetery record may provide additional detail.

The 1901 census places Flora and Ralph in the Shubenacadie household of Isaac and Hannah Barnhill. Flora was recorded as their daughter and as a widow; Ralph was recorded as their grandson.[^6] The census therefore independently confirms Harry's death before 1901, Flora's relationship to the Barnhill household, and Ralph's survival. Its March 10 birth date for Ralph conflicts with the February 11 date in the Groveland register. The contemporaneous record created in Ralph's birth town is presently preferred.[^3][^6]

## Death and burial

![[harry-francis-hopkinson-grave.jpg|525]]

Harry died in Groveland on **November 1, 1897**. The town death transcription describes him as married and gives his age as **29 years and 28 days**.[^4] That age agrees exactly with the interval from his recorded birth on October 4, 1868.

Groveland's annual report records the same death but gives Harry's age as **29 years and 26 days**.[^7] Because the annual report agrees on the death date but conflicts by two days with both the town transcription and the calculation from the birth record, the 26-day figure is treated as a minor copying or calculation error. Both readings are preserved here.

The annual report identifies Groveland as Harry's place of death and burial but does not state a medical cause.[^7] Neither the presently available town transcription nor the annual report answers why he died at twenty-nine. The complete original death return or certificate remains the highest-value missing record.

Harry was buried at **Riverview Cemetery** in Groveland. The town cemetery database places him in **Holly 1, plot 290**, a family plot that also contains records for Lowell H. and Margaret Hopkinson and several other Hopkinson relatives.[^8] Some dates in the cemetery database are incomplete or erroneous, so it is used here for burial location and plot association rather than as the authority for Harry's vital dates.

## Direct-line position

Harry occupies the direct line between his father and son:

**[[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]] → Harry Francis Hopkinson → [[Ralph Lowell Hopkinson Sr.]]**

Through Lowell, Harry inherited the Hopkinson surname and the documented colonial Hopkinson ancestry that passed through [[Ann Matilda Hopkinson]]. The larger ancestry should not be described as an uninterrupted male-line or Y-chromosome Hopkinson descent.

## Evidence assessment

### Confirmed

- Harry was born at Groveland on October 4, 1868, to Lowell H. Hopkinson and Margaret Metcalf.[^1]
- Flora Barnhill was his wife and the mother of Marion and Ralph.[^2][^3][^6]
- Marion was born at Groveland on June 16, 1892, and died at Shubenacadie on February 5, 1898.[^2][^5]
- Ralph was born at Groveland on February 11, 1894; the original entry did not record his given name.[^3]
- Harry died married at Groveland on November 1, 1897.[^4][^7]
- He was buried in Riverview Cemetery, Holly 1, plot 290.[^7][^8]

### Strong inference

- Harry and Flora probably married about 1890 in or through Haverhill, based on the marginal annotation in Harry's birth entry.[^1]
- The $2 tax abatement under Harry's name probably represents cancellation of his poll tax after death.[^7]

### Unverified

- No occupation or street address reported by a secondary source has been accepted without an identifying contemporary record.
- Flora's reported 1864 birth year remains unverified and conflicts with the 1861 year in the 1901 census.[^6]

### Unresolved

- Harry's cause of death
- His occupation and exact residence
- The exact date and full contents of his marriage return
- Whether a contemporary obituary or probate proceeding survives
- The reason for the $2 tax abatement, although a post-death poll-tax cancellation is likely

## Research priorities

1. **Order Harry's complete death record from the Groveland Town Clerk.** Request every field associated with the November 1, 1897 death, including cause, duration of illness, occupation, residence, attending physician or reporter, and burial information.[^10]
2. **Obtain the Haverhill marriage return.** Search approximately 1889–1891 for Harry F. Hopkinson and Flora Barnhill, including intentions and completed returns. The record may establish the exact date, place, residences, occupations, ages, and parents.[^11]
3. **Search contemporary newspapers and special collections.** Focus on Groveland and Haverhill papers published immediately after November 1, 1897. No defensible obituary was identified in the online searches completed for this page, but that limited negative result does not establish that none was printed.
4. **Check Essex County probate indexes.** No defensible online probate entry has been identified. A county-level index or original docket search is still needed before concluding that Harry left no probate estate.
5. **Investigate Marion's Nova Scotia death.** Church, newspaper, and cemetery records are more promising than a provincial civil certificate because of the civil-registration gap.[^9]
