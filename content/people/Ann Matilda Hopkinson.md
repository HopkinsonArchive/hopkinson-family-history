**Ann Matilda Hopkinson** was born on November 15, 1817, in Bradford, Massachusetts, the daughter of [[Uriah Hopkinson]] and [[Inez Lucy Chase|Inez “Lucy” Chase]]. She was the documented mother of [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]] and later the wife of John R. Estabrooks of East Haverhill.

Ann occupies an important generational position in the direct family line. The documented Hopkinson descent and surname passed through her to Lowell, while the project identifies [[William Pillsbury Bailey]] as Lowell’s biological father by strong circumstantial inference. No record found so far directly names Lowell’s father.

## Parents and childhood

Ann was born into the household of Uriah and Lucy Hopkinson in Bradford. She was one of their several children and grew up in the Bradford and Merrimack Valley area.

The surviving evidence establishes her parentage, but no detailed account of her education or childhood has yet been found.

## William Pillsbury Bailey and Lowell’s birth

On May 7, 1836, **William Pillsbury Bailey of West Newbury** and **Ann Matilda Hopkinson of Bradford** filed a formal intention to marry. Ann was then pregnant. Thirty-nine days later, on June 15, she gave birth in Bradford to Lowell Herbert Hopkinson.

No completed marriage between Ann and William has been found. Lowell’s surviving birth record names Ann as his mother but does not identify a father, and Lowell received the Hopkinson surname.

The project treats William as Lowell’s biological father by **strong circumstantial inference** because:

- William and Ann publicly declared their intention to marry during Ann’s pregnancy.
- The intention was filed only thirty-nine days before Lowell’s birth.
- No competing man has been found in the surviving contemporary record.
- Both later married other people after their intended union failed.

This conclusion is stronger than merely describing Lowell’s father as unidentified, but it is not direct proof. No paternity acknowledgment, court proceeding, guardianship record, or birth record naming William has yet been located.

William married Martha A. Chase on April 26, 1838. Ann did not adopt the Bailey surname in the records examined.

## Lowell’s upbringing

Lowell was raised principally in the household of his maternal grandparents, Uriah and Lucy Hopkinson. By the 1850 census, thirteen-year-old Lowell was living with them while Ann maintained a separate household with her later husband and younger son.

This separation of households is documented, but its cause is not. The evidence does not establish whether Lowell began living with Uriah and Lucy immediately after birth or at some later point. It also does not justify describing Ann as having abandoned him or as having no continuing relationship with him.

The most plausible reconstruction is that Uriah and Lucy assumed the principal parental role after Ann’s intended marriage to William Bailey failed, and that Lowell remained with them after Ann entered her later marriage. This remains an inference rather than a recorded custody arrangement.

## Marriage to John R. Estabrooks

Ann Matilda Hopkinson of Bradford and **John R. Estabrooks of East Haverhill** recorded their marriage intention on November 9, 1839. A local Rocks Village historical reconstruction gives their completed marriage date as December 12, 1839, although the original marriage entry has not yet been examined.

John’s middle initial was **R**, not W. His surname appears in several forms, including Estabrook, Estabrooks, Esterbrook, and Easterbrooks.

John was a carpenter and reportedly served as a justice of the peace. Ann and John had at least one documented child together:

- **John L. Estabrook**, born about 1844–1845.

No evidence found so far establishes that Lowell ever became a member of the Estabrook household.

## Recorded households

### 1850 federal census

Ann lived in East Haverhill with:

- John R. Estabrooks, age 36, carpenter;
- Ann M. Estabrooks, age 32;
- John L. Estabrooks, age 5.

Lowell was enumerated separately in Uriah and Lucy Hopkinson’s household.

### 1855 Massachusetts census

The family remained together:

- John R. Estabrook, age 41, carpenter;
- Ann M. Estabrook, age 36;
- John L. Estabrook, age 11.

### 1865 Massachusetts census

After John R.’s death, the household contained:

- Ann M. Estabrook, age 47, widow and **shoe stitcher**;
- John L. Estabrook, age 20, cordwainer.

John L. married Mary E. Elliott in 1865.

## Work in the shoe industry

The 1865 Massachusetts census identified Ann as a **shoe stitcher**. This places her directly within the Merrimack Valley shoe industry that also employed members of her family and community.

Her son John L. was a cordwainer in 1865 and later worked in a shoe factory. Lowell also became a shoemaker. These occupations demonstrate a family and neighborhood connection to shoe production, but they do not establish who taught Lowell the trade.

Ann may have stitched shoe components at home or in a shop, but the census does not identify her work setting. She should not be assigned to a particular factory without further evidence.

## Widowhood, health, and John R.’s estate

John R. Estabrooks died in 1864. His Essex County probate file, case **38586**, named Ann as his widow and an administrator of his estate alongside Samuel Elliott and George E. Elliott. John L., then nineteen, was identified as John’s son.

The estate included multiple parcels and buildings appraised at approximately $3,300. Probate material described Ann as being in **“feeble health and not able to do much for her own support.”** She received a widow’s allowance of $400.

This statement is the only presently known description of Ann’s health. It does not identify a disease or disability. Despite that assessment, the following year’s census recorded her working as a shoe stitcher, possibly in a limited or home-based capacity.

The estate was burdened by a substantial debt of approximately $2,000. Ann, John L., and John L.’s wife Mary participated in later property conveyances connected with resolving the estate’s obligations.

## Final documented years

On April 29, 1867, Ann M. Estabrook, John L. Estabrook, and Mary E. Estabrook conveyed estate property to Lydia A. Davis. On August 14, 1867, the three participated in another mortgage involving former Estabrook land. The August deed is the last record presently found that conclusively establishes Ann was alive.

A deed dated March 30, 1868, described property as adjoining land recorded in Ann M. Estabrook’s name. This may indicate that she was still living or retained a property interest, but boundary descriptions sometimes continued using an earlier proprietor’s name after death or transfer. It is therefore not conclusive evidence that she was alive on that date.

Ann was not found in John L.’s 1870 household. John L. was then living in Haverhill with Mary and their children Albert and Frank. Ann has not been securely identified elsewhere in the 1870 census or in the searchable 1872–1873 directory.

A local reconstruction tentatively gives Ann’s death as **1868?**, apparently based on her disappearance from the later record. No civil death entry, gravestone, burial register, probate estate, newspaper notice, or heirs’ deed has yet confirmed that year.

Her death must therefore remain recorded as **unknown**, with 1868 retained only as an unverified research possibility.

## Direct-line position

The documented Hopkinson line continues through Ann:

[[Uriah Hopkinson]] → **Ann Matilda Hopkinson** → [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]]

The project’s working biological paternal connection is:

[[William Pillsbury Bailey]] → [[Lowell Herbert Hopkinson]]

This is not an uninterrupted male-line or Y-chromosome Hopkinson descent. Lowell inherited the Hopkinson surname through his mother.

## Evidence assessment

### Confirmed

- Ann’s birth on November 15, 1817, in Bradford.
- Her parentage as the daughter of Uriah and Lucy Hopkinson.
- The May 7, 1836 marriage intention with William Pillsbury Bailey.
- Her motherhood of Lowell Herbert Hopkinson.
- Lowell’s use of the Hopkinson surname and principal upbringing by Uriah and Lucy.
- The November 9, 1839 marriage intention with John R. Estabrooks.
- Ann and John’s subsequent marital household.
- Her motherhood of John L. Estabrook.
- Her 1850, 1855, and 1865 household appearances.
- Her occupation as a shoe stitcher in 1865.
- Her status as John R.’s widow and estate administrator.
- Her documented poor health during the probate proceedings.
- Her participation in the April and August 1867 property transactions.

### Strong inference

- William Pillsbury Bailey was Lowell’s biological father.
- Uriah and Lucy assumed the principal parental role after Ann and William’s intended marriage failed.

### Unverified

- December 12, 1839, as the exact date of Ann’s marriage to John R.
- Death in or about 1868.

### Unresolved

- Why Ann and William did not complete their intended marriage.
- When and under what arrangement Lowell entered Uriah and Lucy’s household.
- The precise nature of Ann’s relationship with Lowell after her later marriage.
- The illness or condition behind the probate description of feeble health.
- Ann’s whereabouts after August 1867.
- Her exact death date, death place, cause of death, and burial.

## Research priorities

- Examine Massachusetts death-register images from 1867 onward under all Estabrook spelling variants.
- Locate Greenwood Cemetery and other Rocks Village burial registers.
- Examine the complete image set for Essex County probate case 38586.
- Trace Ann in later Essex County grantor, grantee, and probate indexes.
- Verify the reported December 12, 1839 marriage date in original Bradford, Haverhill, or church records.
- Search Essex County court and town records for a paternity, maintenance, guardianship, or marriage-related proceeding involving Ann and William Bailey.
