# Ralph Lowell Hopkinson Jr.

**Ralph Lowell Hopkinson Jr.**, known at school as **“Hoppy,”** was born in 1920 to [[Ralph Lowell Hopkinson Sr.]] and [[Alma Irene (Palardy) Hopkinson]]. A FamilySearch profile reports an exact birth of **February 2, 1920, at Attleboro**, and displays ten attached sources for the birth conclusion. The saved Details page does not identify or reproduce those sources, so the exact date and place remain **Unverified** until the attached record is examined.[^1][^5]

Ralph completed the Technical course at Attleboro High School in 1938. His senior profile identified him as “Hoppy” and named **“Technical Cadet, United States Maritime Commission”** as his intended destination. That contemporary statement documents a maritime ambition but does not establish that he entered the cadet program or later served in the Merchant Marine, Naval Reserve, or Navy.[^2]

Ralph married his high-school classmate [[Lillian May (Higson) Hopkinson]]. In 1950 he was recorded as a married household head and **jewelry toolmaker** employed in jewelry manufacturing. Ralph and Lillian had four children: two living daughters whose identities are withheld from the public archive, [[Dale Hopkinson]], and [[Ralph Lowell Hopkinson III]]. Ralph died on **December 14, 2000**. FamilySearch reports that the death occurred at **Webster, Worcester County, Massachusetts**, but the underlying death-place source has not yet been inspected.[^3][^4][^5][^6][^7]

## Family and relationships

### Parents

Ralph was the documented son of Ralph Lowell Hopkinson Sr. and Alma Irene Palardy Hopkinson. The North Purchase Cemetery family monument explicitly identifies Ralph Jr. as their son. The FamilySearch person profile independently places him in the same parental family.[^1][^5]

### Marriage to Lillian May Higson

Ralph and Lillian were both members of Attleboro High School's class of 1938. A humorous class-prophecy passage described Ralph, a “prominent DeMolayan,” in a “return engagement” with “Lil Higson,” showing that their relationship was already known before graduation.[^2]

Lillian's obituary identifies Ralph as her husband of sixty years and states that he died on December 14, 2000. The duration places their marriage at approximately 1940, but no original marriage return has yet been examined. The FamilySearch profile contains no marriage event for the couple; that absence does not establish that no record exists.[^3][^5]

Lillian's obituary also reports that Ralph and Lillian served together as co-chairs of the annual bazaar and other fundraising committees at All Saints Episcopal Church.[^3]

## Early life and education

Ralph grew up in the Attleboro area. FamilySearch contains residence entries for Attleboro in 1930 and South Attleboro in 1935 and 1940, but those entries display no attached sources in the saved page and therefore remain unverified leads.[^5]

The 1938 Attleboro High School *Tattletale* provides the clearest contemporary account of Ralph as a young man. It identifies him as a Technical-course graduate, gives the nickname “Hoppy,” and describes his intended destination as the United States Maritime Commission's technical-cadet program. The yearbook also calls him a prominent DeMolayan and links him socially with Lillian Higson.[^2]

## Residences, households, and property

Attleboro was Ralph's principal documented community during his youth and family life. The 1950 census places the family in Attleboro, although the supplied transcription does not include the street address. Ralph III's obituary states that he was born and raised in Attleboro, reinforcing the family's continued presence there during the 1950s.[^4][^6]

First-hand family testimony identifies **6 Benefit Street, Attleboro**, as property owned by Ralph and Lillian. The address should be treated as a documented family-associated property based on direct testimony, while deed, assessor, directory, or census evidence remains desirable for the public record. The separate “6 High Street” reference previously encountered in Ralph Sr.'s research is an error and is not retained as a possible address.[^8]

The FamilySearch profile reports “Previous Residence” at **Livingston, Polk County, Texas**, but the entry has no attached source on the saved page. It may reflect a late-life residence, mailing address, Social Security record, or another administrative field. It does not establish that Ralph died in Texas.[^5]

## Occupation and economic life

On April 6, 1950, Ralph was recorded as a **jewelry toolmaker** working in the jewelry-manufacturing industry. This is the first occupation presently established by a contemporary federal record. His employer, earlier work, later occupational history, and retirement remain unresolved.[^6]

## Maritime and military service

Ralph's 1938 yearbook identified **“Technical Cadet, United States Maritime Commission”** as his intended destination. The cadet program trained officers for the United States Merchant Marine and could include naval-science instruction or reserve pathways, but it was institutionally distinct from direct United States Navy service.[^2][^9]

First-hand family testimony remembers Ralph as having served in the **United States Navy**. The testimony is retained as an **Unverified** service claim because no identifying personnel file, discharge, ship roster, muster roll, service number, or veterans' record has yet been found. The present evidence does not determine whether the memory refers to direct Navy service, Naval Reserve status, a Merchant Marine Reserve connection, Merchant Marine employment, or another maritime role.[^8]

The frontmatter field `military_service` is therefore left blank rather than set to `false`. No military tag is applied until an identifying record establishes the service category.

## Community and organizational life

The 1938 yearbook's description of Ralph as a prominent DeMolayan documents an association with the Order of DeMolay, although the chapter, initiation date, offices, and length of membership remain unknown.[^2]

Lillian's obituary records that Ralph and Lillian jointly chaired the annual bazaar and other fundraising committees at All Saints Episcopal Church. This establishes public community activity by the couple, although the exact years remain unresolved.[^3]

## Death and burial
![[Ralph-Lowell-Hopkinson-Sr.-Grave.jpg]]
Ralph's death on **December 14, 2000**, is supported by Lillian's obituary and the FamilySearch profile and is therefore treated as **Confirmed**.[^3][^5]

FamilySearch reports the death place as **Webster, Worcester County, Massachusetts**, and displays six linked sources for the death conclusion. Because the saved Details page does not identify those sources, Webster remains an **Unverified** reported place rather than a fully confirmed conclusion. Ralph's original death certificate or obituary should resolve his death place, residence, occupation, and cause of death.[^5]

Ralph's cause of death remains unresolved. No inference should be made from Dale's ALS or from other family neurological history without evidence concerning Ralph himself.

The family monument and FamilySearch profile associate Ralph with **North Purchase Cemetery** in Attleboro. Section C, lot 133 is retained as a strong working conclusion from the cemetery evidence already developed for Ralph Sr., pending the cemetery burial register or lot card.[^1][^5][^8]
