**Ralph Lowell Hopkinson Sr.** was born at Groveland, Massachusetts, on February 11, 1894, the son of [[people/Harry Francis Hopkinson|Harry Francis Hopkinson]] and [[people/Flora Barnhill|Flora Barnhill]]. The original Groveland register records the birth of an unnamed child to Harry F. and Flora Hopkinson; Ralph's identity is established by the matching date, parents, place, and later records.[^1]

Ralph lost his father in 1897, when he was three. His older sister, Marion Maude Hopkinson, died at Shubenacadie, Nova Scotia, in 1898. By 1901, Ralph and his widowed mother were living in the Shubenacadie household of Flora's parents, Isaac and Hannah Barnhill.[^2] He therefore spent at least part of his childhood within his maternal family in Nova Scotia before returning to Massachusetts.

By the First World War era Ralph was established in Attleboro. He registered for the United States draft there, married Alma Irene Palardy on Christmas Day 1918 according to an indexed Massachusetts marriage record, and became a permanent member of the Attleboro Fire Department by 1925.[^4][^5][^6] The family later lived at 6 Benefit Street, the address associated with Ralph, Alma, Flora, and the succeeding Hopkinson household in contemporary records and family testimony.[^7][^9][^10][^13]

## Family and relationships

### Parents and sister

Ralph was the younger documented child of Harry Francis Hopkinson and Flora Barnhill. His sister, Marion Maude Hopkinson, was born in 1892 and died at Shubenacadie in 1898. Harry died at Groveland on November 1, 1897. The sequence left Flora widowed with Ralph as her surviving known child.[^1][^2]

### Marriage to Alma Irene Palardy

An indexed Massachusetts marriage entry records Ralph Lowell Hopkinson's marriage to **Alma Irene Palardy Morrisseau** at Attleboro on December 25, 1918, certificate number 112, page 450.[^5] The North Purchase Cemetery monument identifies her more simply as **Alma I. Palardy**, born in 1891 and deceased in 1970, and explicitly calls her Ralph's wife.[^11]

The two forms of Alma's name must both be preserved. The indexed form may indicate that *Morrisseau* was a former married surname, an alternate surname, or a feature of the underlying record, but the original marriage certificate has not yet been examined. Until it is, her public page name is **Alma Irene Palardy Hopkinson**, with *Morrisseau* retained as an indexed alternate name rather than silently discarded.

## Life and historical context

### Early childhood in Groveland and Nova Scotia

Ralph's earliest years were marked by two family deaths. His father died when Ralph was three, and his sister died the following year. The 1901 Canadian census then placed Ralph, aged seven, with his mother in his maternal grandparents' home at Shubenacadie.[^2]

The census gives Ralph's birth as March 10, 1894. A later World War I draft index gives February 11, 1895.[^2][^4] Both conflict with the original Groveland register, which records February 11, 1894 and is preferred because it was created in Ralph's birth town close to the event.[^1]

### Return to Attleboro

The exact date of Ralph's return from Nova Scotia has not been found. Flora was documented at 19 Morey Street in Attleboro by December 31, 1913, while Ralph's own presence is securely indicated by his Attleboro draft registration and 1918 marriage.[^3][^4][^5] The evidence therefore places his return after the 1901 census and no later than the First World War registration period.

A 1943 municipal index rendered Ralph's address as **6 High Street**. That entry is treated as an error. Repeated family records and documented family testimony identify the property as **6 Benefit Street**, and the 1946 municipal entry uses Benefit Street. High Street should not be presented as a separate or competing residence.[^9]

## Occupation and economic life

### Attleboro Fire Department

Ralph developed a documented career in the Attleboro Fire Department. He does not appear on the published 1924 permanent or call rosters, but the 1925 annual report lists **Ralph L. Hopkinson** among the permanent men assigned to the Union Street Station and gives his residence as 98 North Avenue.[^6] The change narrows his entry into permanent fire service to the period between those rosters, although the exact appointment date remains unresolved.

Later municipal records continued to identify him with the department and with 6 Benefit Street.[^7] In October 1944 the city recorded a transfer of **$210 for “Ralph Hopkinson Pension.”**[^8] The wording establishes a municipal pension transaction in his name but does not, by itself, provide the complete retirement date, pension terms, or reason for leaving active service.

A city entry dated April 16, 1946 concerns Ralph Hopkinson of 6 Benefit Street in connection with a hackney-carriage matter.[^9] The record indicates activity outside or after his principal fire-service career, but the surviving index alone does not establish the nature or duration of any transportation business.

## Military and public service

Ralph registered for the United States draft at Attleboro during the 1917–1918 registration period. The indexed record identifies him as a United States citizen born at Groveland, although it records the birth year incorrectly as 1895.[^4]

Draft registration does not establish induction or military service. No verified enlistment, unit, rank, discharge, veterans' organization, or Massachusetts service card has yet been connected to Ralph. His military-service status therefore remains **unresolved**, and no military tag is applied to this page.

His confirmed public service was municipal: he served as a permanent Attleboro firefighter and later appears in a city pension transaction.[^6][^7][^8]

## Death and burial

![[Ralph-Lowell-Hopkinson-Sr.-Grave.jpg]]

Ralph died in 1946 at the age of fifty-two. A later cemetery memorial reports May 28, 1946 at Attleboro, and the *Attleboro Sun* published his obituary on that date.[^10][^12] Because the original death certificate and a clean image of the complete obituary have not yet been examined, the exact date and place are classified as a **strong inference** rather than confirmed.

The Attleboro newspaper archive locates an obituary on page 1 of its May 28, 1946 issue, but the saved OCR is damaged and the complete article has not yet been transcribed.[^10] Ralph's parentage is independently established by the Groveland birth record, while his cause of death remains unresolved.[^1]

Ralph was buried at **North Purchase Cemetery** in Attleboro. The photographed family monument places him with his mother Flora, his wife Alma, his son Ralph Jr., and his daughter-in-law Lillian M. Higson.[^11] The Find a Grave memorial identifies the location as **section C, lot 133**.[^12]

## Direct-line position

Ralph occupies the documented direct line between his father and son:

Through Harry and Lowell Herbert Hopkinson, Ralph inherited the Hopkinson surname and the documented colonial Hopkinson ancestry that passed through [[people/Ann Matilda Hopkinson|Ann Matilda Hopkinson]]. ## Evidence assessment

### Confirmed

- Ralph was born at Groveland on February 11, 1894, to Harry F. Hopkinson and Flora Barnhill.[^1]
- In 1901 he lived at Shubenacadie with his widowed mother and maternal grandparents.[^2]
- Alma I. Palardy was his wife, and Ralph L. Hopkinson Jr. was their documented son.[^5][^11]
- Ralph was a permanent member of the Attleboro Fire Department by 1925.[^6]
- The established family residence was 6 Benefit Street; “6 High Street” is an erroneous indexed address.[^7][^9][^10]
- He was buried at North Purchase Cemetery in the family lot.[^11][^12]

### Strong inference

- Ralph returned from Nova Scotia to Massachusetts after 1901 and no later than the First World War draft-registration period.[^2][^4]
- He married Alma at Attleboro on December 25, 1918, as reported by the indexed marriage record.[^5]
- He died at Attleboro on May 28, 1946.[^10][^12]
- The transition between the 1924 and 1925 rosters indicates that his permanent fire appointment occurred during that interval.[^6]

### Unverified

- The meaning of *Morrisseau* in Alma's indexed marriage name has not been determined.[^5]
- The Find a Grave section and lot designation has not yet been compared with a cemetery lot card or burial register.[^12]

### Unresolved

- Ralph's exact return date from Nova Scotia
- His complete original marriage certificate and the explanation of Alma's indexed surname *Morrisseau*
- Whether Ralph and Alma had any child in addition to Ralph Jr.
- His exact fire-department appointment and retirement dates
- The full terms and basis of the 1944 pension transaction
- Whether he performed military service after registering for the draft
- His original death certificate and cause of death
- A clean transcription of his complete 1946 obituary

Earlier summaries left Ralph's spouse, occupation, exact death, obituary, and burial largely unresolved. The marriage index, Attleboro municipal records, contemporary obituary, family monument, and cemetery memorial now identify Alma, document his fire-service career, and establish his burial while preserving the remaining evidentiary limits.

## Research priorities

1. **Obtain the original 1918 Attleboro marriage certificate.** Request certificate 112, page 450, for Ralph Lowell Hopkinson and Alma Irene Palardy Morrisseau. It should clarify Alma's marital status and surname history, both spouses' residences, occupations, parents, and whether the marriage was a first or subsequent marriage.[^5]
2. **Obtain Ralph's original death certificate.** Request the May 1946 Attleboro record to confirm the date, place, residence, occupation, cause and duration of death, informant, and burial details.
3. **Examine the complete May 28, 1946 obituary image.** A clean reading may supply the wife, survivors, fire-service history, funeral arrangements, and organizational affiliations.[^10]
4. **Complete the military-service check.** Compare the original draft card with Massachusetts World War I service cards, induction records, and discharge indexes. Registration alone must not be treated as service.[^4]
5. **Locate the Fire Department appointment and pension records.** Seek personnel ledgers, appointment votes, retirement proceedings, and pension files to establish the precise career span and reason for pensioning.[^6][^7][^8]
6. **Verify the cemetery lot record.** Obtain the North Purchase Cemetery lot card or burial register for section C, lot 133.[^12]
7. **Test for additional children.** Examine the 1920, 1930, and 1940 census households, Attleboro birth records, and the complete obituaries of Ralph and Alma.
