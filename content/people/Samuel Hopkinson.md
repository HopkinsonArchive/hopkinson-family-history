> [!summary] Profile
> **Born:** July 14, 1718, Bradford, Massachusetts Bay Province  
> **Died:** February 1, 1785, Bradford, Massachusetts  
> **Buried:** Ancient Burying Ground at East Bradford, now Riverview Cemetery, Groveland  
> **Parents:** [[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]] and [[Martha Spofford]]  
> **Spouse:** [[Betty Palmer]]  
> **Direct-line child:** [[Silas Hopkinson]]  
> **Evidence status:** Confirmed

Samuel Hopkinson was a lifelong resident of Bradford and a son of [[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]] and [[Martha Spofford]]. He married [[Betty Palmer]] in 1741, and the Bradford records identify twelve children born to their family. Samuel was known locally as **Ensign Samuel Hopkinson**, indicating that he held commissioned rank in the local militia. During the Revolutionary War he was also selected to help Bradford obtain soldiers needed to meet the town's military obligations.

## Birth and parentage

The Bradford vital record identifies Samuel directly as the son of Caleb Jr. and Martha Hopkinson and records his birth on **July 14, 1718**.

This resolves a conflict in later online accounts that give June 14, 1718. The published Bradford record supports July 14.

Samuel was the seventh recorded child of Caleb Jr. and Martha. He was twelve years old when his father died in a cart accident on November 9, 1730. His mother later married [[Ebenezer Kimball]] on June 5, 1732.

**Evidence status: Confirmed.**

## Marriage to Betty Palmer

Samuel married [[Betty Palmer]] in Bradford on **May 28, 1741**. The marriage record describes both parties as residents of Bradford.

Betty was born January 6, 1724/25 in Bradford and was a daughter of [[Samuel Palmer]] and [[Elizabeth Green Palmer]]. Her father was a Bradford yeoman. Betty survived Samuel by almost thirty years and died from old age on January 17, 1815, aged ninety. The East Bradford church record then described her as Samuel's widow.

**Evidence status: Marriage and Betty's death confirmed; her parentage is strongly supported by Bradford and Palmer family records.**

## Children

The Bradford vital records identify the following children of Samuel and Betty:

1. **Betty Hopkinson**, born January 21, 1741/42; married Stephen Burbank in 1757.
2. **Judah or Judith Hopkinson**, born December 29, 1743; later records call her Judith; married Nathaniel Parker Jr. on March 29, 1768.
3. **Hannah Hopkinson**, born December 4, 1745; married Silvanus Lowell on August 2, 1770.
4. **Abigail Hopkinson**, born February 3, 1747; died October 22 or 25, 1771.
5. **Samuel Hopkinson Jr.**, born February 20, 1749; died November 2, 1771.
6. **Ebenezer Hopkinson**, born May 26, 1752.
7. **Paul Hopkinson**, born October 15, 1754; died December 23, 1771.
8. **Alles or Alice Hopkinson**, born July 13, 1757; married Daniel Nichols in 1775.
9. **Rachel Hopkinson**, born April 3, 1760; married Phineas Hardy in 1789.
10. **Aaron Hopkinson**, baptized March 28, 1762; died during April 1762.
11. **[[Silas Hopkinson]]**, born May 22, 1763; next direct-line ancestor.
12. **Sarah Hopkinson**, born November 5, 1767.

This sequence corrects a faulty later genealogy that placed Silas's birth in 1767 and Sarah's in 1769. The Bradford record explicitly identifies Silas as the son of Samuel and Betty and gives his birth as **May 22, 1763**.

The daughter's birth name appears as **Judah** in the compiled Bradford birth record but as **Judith** in her marriage and later records. Both forms are preserved here rather than silently choosing one.

**Evidence status: Confirmed through the Bradford vital-record compilation; individual original entries should be inspected where available.**

## Three children lost in 1771

Samuel and Betty experienced an extraordinary concentration of family deaths during the final months of 1771:

| Child | Approximate age | Death |
|---|---:|---:|
| Abigail | 24 | October 22 or 25, 1771 |
| Samuel Jr. | 22 | November 2, 1771 |
| Paul | 17 | December 23, 1771 |

The church record dates Abigail's death to October 25, while her gravestone was transcribed as October 22. Both dates are retained.

No Bradford marriages or separate households have been found for Abigail, Samuel Jr., or Paul. Paul was still a minor, and all three probably remained in their parents' East Bradford household. Their deaths within approximately sixty-two days are therefore treated in this archive as probably resulting from a **shared household illness or exposure**.

Bradford's surviving records do not show a comparable town-wide concentration of deaths during the same period, and local histories do not identify 1771 as one of the town's recognized epidemic years. Serious throat illnesses described as angina or throat distemper circulated in parts of colonial America, but no record identifies the disease or other cause responsible for the Hopkinson deaths.

**Evidence status: Deaths and family relationships confirmed; common household and shared cause strongly inferred; particular cause unresolved.**

## Ensign in the Bradford militia

Samuel's gravestone and the gravestones of his children repeatedly style him **Ensign Samuel Hopkinson**. Ensign was a junior commissioned militia rank, traditionally associated with carrying and protecting a company's colors.

A later genealogy states that Samuel was an ensign in the Bradford company and “probably” served actively during the French and Indian Wars. No muster roll or commission proving such service has yet been identified. The rank is confirmed, but the date of his commission, the particular company, and any field service remain unresolved.

Samuel has not been identified as one of the Bradford men who fought at Bunker Hill. The family's Bunker Hill tradition should not be assigned to him without a specific service record.

**Evidence status: Ensign title confirmed; French and Indian War service unverified; personal Revolutionary field service unverified.**

## Revolutionary-era civic service

At a Bradford town meeting on June 28, 1780, the inhabitants voted to obtain four men for six months of Continental Army service and nineteen men for three months of militia service. Samuel and a David Hopkinson—probably Samuel's older brother [[David Hopkinson]]—were among a large committee selected to procure the required soldiers.

Samuel was then nearly sixty-two years old. The appointment documents his direct participation in Bradford's wartime administration and suggests that the town considered him a dependable inhabitant. It does not mean that he was one of the men hired or that he personally served in the field during the Revolution.

**Evidence status: Confirmed through Bradford town proceedings.**

## Probate

Samuel made a will on **June 24, 1784**. His estate was processed in Essex County as **Probate Case 13,922**, with proceedings dated March 7, 1785. The estate is indexed as testate, confirming that Samuel left a will.

The complete probate packet has not yet been examined. It may identify Samuel's occupation, property, executor, surviving heirs, debts, household goods, and the portion assigned to [[Silas Hopkinson]]. No conclusions about those details should be made until the original file is inspected.

**Evidence status: Probate case and testate status identified; contents unresolved.**

## Death and burial

Samuel died in Bradford on **February 1, 1785**. His East Bradford gravestone was transcribed as:

> Erected in Memory of Ensign  
> Samuel Hopkinson  
> who Departed this  
> Life February the 1st  
> A.D. 1785 in the 67th  
> year of his Age

The wording “in the 67th year of his age” means that Samuel was sixty-six and had entered his sixty-seventh year. That agrees with his documented July 14, 1718 birth.

The burial ground was originally the Ancient Burying Ground at East Bradford. After East Bradford became Groveland in 1850, it became known as Riverview Cemetery.

Betty survived Samuel and continued to be identified as his widow. No subsequent marriage has been found.

**Evidence status: Confirmed.**

## Historical assessment

The surviving evidence presents Samuel as a settled eighteenth-century Bradford householder with a large family, commissioned militia standing, and a role in the town's Revolutionary mobilization. The records also preserve an unusually personal family tragedy: the probable shared household event that killed three of his children during the closing months of 1771.

The direct line continues through his son Silas:

**[[Caleb Hopkinson Jr.]] → Samuel Hopkinson → [[Silas Hopkinson]]**

## Evidence summary

### Confirmed

- Son of Caleb Hopkinson Jr. and Martha Spofford
- Born July 14, 1718 in Bradford
- Married Betty Palmer on May 28, 1741
- Father of twelve children recorded in Bradford
- Father of Silas Hopkinson, born May 22, 1763
- Known as Ensign Samuel Hopkinson
- Selected for Bradford's 1780 committee to obtain Continental and militia soldiers
- Made a will in 1784
- Estate indexed as Essex County Probate Case 13,922
- Died February 1, 1785
- Buried in the Ancient Burying Ground at East Bradford

### Strong inference

- Abigail, Samuel Jr., and Paul remained in Samuel and Betty's household in 1771
- Their closely clustered deaths resulted from a shared household illness or exposure

### Unresolved

- Samuel's occupation
- The location and extent of his Bradford property
- The specific militia company in which he served as ensign
- Date and circumstances of his commission
- Whether he served actively during the French and Indian Wars
- Whether he performed personal field service during the Revolution
- The particular cause of the three deaths in 1771
- Complete contents of his will and probate estate

## Research priorities

1. Retrieve and transcribe Essex County Probate Case 13,922.
2. Search Essex County deeds for Samuel's property and transactions.
3. Locate his militia commission or an identifiable Bradford company roll.
4. Search French and Indian War muster rolls for a matching Samuel Hopkinson.
5. Examine the original Bradford birth, marriage, church, and death entries.
6. Investigate the present location of the Hopkinson family Bible records cited as Bradford Private Records 13 and 22.
7. Search surviving ministers' diaries or family papers for the cause of the 1771 deaths.
